# 05 — Trust, privacy, and proof

## Trust-centre content model

Create a trust centre when the evidence exists. It should contain:

- hosting and data-residency options;
- encryption in transit and at rest;
- identity, role, matter, and tenant isolation;
- retention, deletion, backups, and recovery;
- subprocessors and model providers;
- provider training/retention policies;
- audit logs and approval history;
- incident response and notification process;
- access reviews and employee offboarding;
- penetration testing and certification status;
- service availability and support model;
- data-processing agreement and security questionnaire pack.

## Product controls to show publicly

- firm-level and matter-level permissions;
- selectable model/provider policies;
- approved channels and integrations;
- human approval before consequential external actions;
- source/provenance visibility;
- configurable retention;
- administrator activity review;
- disabled actions and explicit “never” boundaries.

## Claims requiring evidence before publication

- no-training-on-customer-data statements;
- “no cross-firm knowledge” or tenant-isolation claims;
- on-premise/local inference capability;
- named integrations;
- security certifications and testing;
- uptime and support response times;
- performance, token-volume, pull-request, or customer-count metrics;
- salary comparisons and ROI calculations;
- legal-regulatory conclusions;
- any customer logo, quote, or case-study result.

## Proof ladder

Use the strongest available proof in this order:

1. named customer case study with permission;
2. quantified pilot outcome with methodology;
3. workflow demonstration using clearly labelled fictional data;
4. architecture/process documentation;
5. founder/team experience;
6. future roadmap language.

Do not use badges as a substitute for controls. Never display certifications that have not been awarded.

## Regulatory tone

Localise legal and compliance language with counsel. The site can say Nella is designed to support confidentiality, oversight, and professional responsibility; it should not imply that using Nella automatically makes a firm compliant.
