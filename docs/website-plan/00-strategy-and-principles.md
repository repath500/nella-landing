# 00 — Strategy and principles

## Website objective

Turn qualified law-firm visitors into private consultations with Nella. The site must help a managing partner, operations lead, IT/security lead, or innovation lead answer four questions quickly:

1. What does Nella actually do for our team?
2. Why is it safer and more appropriate than a generic AI subscription?
3. How much control do we retain?
4. What happens after we book a conversation?

## Strategic translation from the source brief

Keep these ideas:

- a private assistant built around the firm;
- existing channels and systems rather than another app;
- firm-specific onboarding and process configuration;
- graduated autonomy with approval controls;
- private cloud, customer cloud, or local deployment;
- model/provider choice;
- strong confidentiality, auditability, and human oversight;
- a managed implementation rather than self-serve software.

Change the framing:

- Nella is the company and trusted partner;
- “assistant” describes the service experience, not a mascot-led brand;
- Nella supports lawyers and staff but never presents itself as a lawyer or legal adviser;
- the website does not depend on the name Anna being understood;
- implementation, governance, and operating discipline are part of the offer.

## Audience priorities

### Primary buyers

- managing partners and practice leads;
- firm operations and knowledge-management leads;
- IT, security, and compliance decision-makers;
- innovation leads evaluating practical AI adoption.

### Influencers and users

- solicitors, attorneys, legal executives, paralegals, and support staff;
- finance and procurement;
- risk, data-protection, and client-relationship teams.

## Message hierarchy

1. Nella gives your team more capable support around the work.
2. Nella is built around your firm, systems, permissions, and processes.
3. Your firm controls where data runs, which models are allowed, and what can happen automatically.
4. Nella implements, manages, measures, and improves the system with you.

## Claims discipline

Every claim must be tagged internally as one of: verified fact, current capability, planned capability, illustrative example, or aspiration. Avoid invented customer logos, certifications, ROI guarantees, security absolutes, and legal advice claims.
